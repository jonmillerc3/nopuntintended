DRAFT DAY RECAP

Money Was Spent. Regrets Were Made.

Ten managers, one auction, and a salary cap that at least one of you apparently forgot existed. Here's what happened.

Team by Team

In no particular order, because ranking these before a single game has been played would be its own kind of dumb.

Estes Park Rangers

Jon Miller · Defending Champion
Kept: Jahmyr Gibbs $41 · Chris Olave $8 · Parker Washington $1

The defending champion doubled down at running back, adding Derrick Henry ($46) and Ashton Jeanty ($32) to a room that already had Jahmyr Gibbs. Three feature-caliber backs on one roster is either the blueprint for a repeat or a very expensive bye-week headache waiting to happen. DeVonta Smith ($31) gives the passing game a real WR1, which matters, because outside of him and Christian Watson the receiver room is thin enough to see through.

Hawkeye Inside Her

David Eickholt
Kept: Ja'Marr Chase $62 · Amon-Ra St. Brown $53 · Jonathan Taylor $27

Spent $142 of the budget on three keepers before the draft even opened, then filled the rest of the roster with whatever was left standing. Chase, St. Brown, and Taylor is as good a top three as exists anywhere in this league, but the depth chart behind them reads like a waiver wire. Also worth noting: rostered Jordan Love for a dollar. A Packers fan taking a flier on the Packers quarterback for a buck is either loyalty or an addiction, and it's probably both.

ThunderFunk

Tom Ksobiech
Kept: Colston Loveland $1 · Brock Bowers $2 · De'Von Achane $27

Kept Colston Loveland for a single dollar, which might be the best value on any roster in this league, then added Brock Bowers behind him, giving ThunderFunk two elite tight ends and not a ton of proven help throwing them the ball outside of Drake London. Also somehow rostered both Patrick Mahomes and Dak Prescott for $6 apiece, a quarterback room built entirely out of the bargain bin.

Bear Force One

Chris Shipley
Kept: Josh Allen $29 · Bijan Robinson $56 · Kyle Pitts Sr. $4

Paid $56 to keep Bijan Robinson, tied for the single biggest number anyone put on a player all offseason, then kept spending, adding a second unproven rookie back in Jeremiah Love for $26. If both hit, this is the best roster in the league. If either one doesn't, it's a very expensive lesson about the rookie basket.

Manatt Mixers

jon manatt
Kept: George Pickens $18 · Rashee Rice $13 · Trey McBride $8

Built a genuinely deep receiver room around Pickens, Rice, Malik Nabers, and Garrett Wilson, then spent what was left on a running back committee nobody will remember by Thanksgiving. Jalen Hurts gives them a rushing floor at quarterback most teams here don't have. Last year's volatility didn't get any less volatile. It just got more expensive.

Sloan

Eric Sloan
Kept: Omarion Hampton $27 · Emeka Egbuka $12 · Chase Brown $3

Paid $56 for Puka Nacua, the other half of this year's biggest-bid tie, and built a receiver room deep enough to field two starting lineups: Nacua, CeeDee Lamb, Terry McLaurin, Zay Flowers, Egbuka. The running back room behind Hampton is Jordan Mason and a prayer.

Sam LaPortaPottys

Josh Ostrem
Kept: Kyren Williams $17 · Chris Godwin Jr. $4 · Drake Maye $3

Spent big on a genuine three-headed monster in Saquon Barkley ($45), A.J. Brown ($37), and Nico Collins ($35), which is a legitimately strong foundation.

Auction Drafts are Dumb

Dan Welter
Kept: Bo Nix $1 · James Cook III $17 · Jaxon Smith-Njigba $40

Landed Christian McCaffrey for $46 and kept Jaxon Smith-Njigba for $40, a strong top of the roster. Then, in the single funniest sentence in this entire recap, finished the draft with $37 in unspent salary cap. A team named "Auction Drafts are Dumb" left more money on the table than several other rosters spent on their entire bench combined. The format was never the dumb part.

Cooper Dejean Jacket

Mike Stout
Kept: Cam Skattebo $6 · Matthew Stafford $1 · Ladd McConkey $5

Assembled maybe the deepest receiver room in the league: Justin Jefferson, Davante Adams, Rome Odunze, Jameson Williams, McConkey. Genuinely impressive. The running back room is Skattebo, Bhayshul Tuten, Kyle Monangai, and MarShawn Lloyd, a committee with no clear lead back, which is a real problem in a league that will not stop needing running backs.

WE WIN RINGS

Lawrence Moffett
Kept: Justin Herbert $5 · Jaylen Waddle $12 · Travis Etienne Jr. $5

Two straight seasons in the basement bought exactly zero humility. WE WIN RINGS quietly built the most balanced roster in the league: two startable quarterbacks in Herbert and Lamar Jackson, a real running back room in Breece Hall, Quinshon Judkins, and Etienne, and a receiver corps led by Waddle and Tee Higgins. On paper, this is a top-three team. On the actual scoreboard, it's WE WIN RINGS, so treat all of the above with appropriate suspicion.

The Not-Actually-Official Draft Awards

No trophy, no cash value, no basis in reality beyond a spreadsheet. Fully deserved anyway.

Best Overall Value
Colston Loveland — $1 (ThunderFunk)

An ascending young tight end for a single dollar. Somewhere, an ESPN default bid of $0 is laughing at everyone who overpaid at the position.

Left Money on the Table
Auction Drafts are Dumb — $37 unspent

A team named after how dumb auction drafts are just proved the format works fine when you actually use the whole budget.

Keeper Tax Bracket
Hawkeye Inside Her — $142 on three keepers

Spent more on keeping three players than four other teams spent building their entire rosters. Chase and St. Brown better both stay healthy.

Committee Meeting Award
Cooper Dejean Jacket

Four running backs, zero clear starters. Skattebo, Tuten, Monangai, and Lloyd are going to need a group chat just to sort out touches.

Biggest Bid of the Draft
Bijan Robinson & Puka Nacua — $56 each

Bear Force One and Sloan both blinked at exactly the same number. Neither one is allowed to complain if the other guy's pick busts first.
