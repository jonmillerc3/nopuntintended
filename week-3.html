REGULAR SEASON · WEEK 3

The $37 Competitive Balance Initiative

Two unbeaten teams, two first wins, and one unsuccessful attempt to make things fair with $37.

We may owe Dan Welter an apology.

Not an actual apology, obviously. Something less legally binding. A respectful acknowledgment that the man we mocked for leaving $37 at the auction has now led the league in scoring two weeks in a row.

Daejon Loves followed 168.58 with 164.72, bringing Dan to 447.94 points through three weeks. That is the most points scored this season by anyone who left at least $37 in draft capital unused.

An admittedly specialized category. An undisputed champion.

Perhaps Dan wasn't forgetting to spend the money. Perhaps he was intentionally trying to even the field and make this fair for the rest of us.

If so, we appreciate the gesture. We would appreciate it considerably more if it were working.

Elsewhere, Estes Park and Sloan are the last two undefeated teams. Cooper Dejean Jacket and WE WIN RINGS finally joined the win column. Hawkeye Inside Her and Bear Force One lost their perfect records. ThunderFunk and Sam LaPortaPottys are now 0-3.

Three weeks in, the league has two teams that haven't lost, two that haven't won, and one man apparently operating a voluntary salary-cap relief program.

Week 3 Matchups

Estes Park Rangers 159.40 · Hawkeye Inside Her 116.38

Last week, Estes scored 159.68.

This week, Estes scored 159.40.

A decline of 0.28 points. We will monitor this alarming collapse.

The defending champion is 3-0, leads the league with 496.20 points, and just handed another previously undefeated team a 43.02-point loss.

Jahmyr Gibbs did most of the early explaining with 41.4 points. Tyler Shough added 23.8, Derrick Henry supplied 21.9, and Chris Olave contributed 19.7. Those four alone produced 106.8.

That is a useful foundation for an afternoon in which the 49ers defense scored zero and Dalton Kincaid managed 3.8.

Jon Miller also had Christian Watson's 22.6 on the bench. Last week it was DeVonta Smith's 27.7. The weekly tradition of leaving a perfectly good receiver performance unused remains intact.

David Eickholt's stars, meanwhile, discovered that last week's points do not roll over.

Ja'Marr Chase scored 24.8 and Joe Burrow added 22.58. But Jonathan Taylor fell from 29.2 to 9.2, and Amon-Ra St. Brown went from 35.2 to 11.9.

The Taylor–Amon-Ra combination lost 43.3 points from one week to the next. Hawkeye lost this matchup by 43.02.

That is not the entire explanation, but it is a remarkably efficient place to start.

Hawkeye is still 2-1 with 404.32 points. One loss to the league's scoring leader is not a crisis. It is, however, an excellent reminder that the elite-stars strategy works best when the elite stars are feeling cooperative.

Final: Estes Park Rangers 159.40, Hawkeye Inside Her 116.38

Cooper Dejean Jacket 140.50 · Bear Force One 110.86

Mike Stout's weekly scoring progression now reads 92.20, 130.38, 140.50.

That is actual improvement. This week, the schedule finally agreed to recognize it.

Cooper Dejean Jacket picked up its first win behind George Kittle's 26.2, Matthew Golden's 21.0, Matthew Stafford's 20.9 and Davante Adams' 20.7.

Four players at 20 or better, and Justin Jefferson wasn't one of them.

Jefferson scored 5.2. Mike won by 29.64 anyway. For a roster that opened the season asking one receiver to operate the whole business, this counts as organizational progress.

Even the Texans defense contributed 10. Two weeks removed from the minus-four masterpiece, the defense appears to have learned which direction the numbers are supposed to go.

Chris Shipley got 35.3 from Bijan Robinson and 21.9 from Jeremiah Love. That should have been the beginning of a very good afternoon.

Instead, it was most of the afternoon.

Josh Allen came back to earth with 17.46 after last week's 40.82. Tetairoa McMillan scored 3.7, Kyle Pitts 1.5, and the Eagles defense contributed minus one.

Bear's scores have now gone 154.16 → 122.02 → 110.86.

Last week, Allen carried everyone home. This week, Bijan picked up the backpack and discovered it contained several bricks.

Bear Force One is 2-1. Cooper is 1-2. The records still favor Chris; the direction of travel has become a considerably less comfortable conversation.

Final: Cooper Dejean Jacket 140.50, Bear Force One 110.86

Daejon Loves 164.72 · Manatt Mixers 150.32

Welcome to the league's newest competitive division: teams whose managers chose to leave at least $37 unspent.

Dan Welter is first.

The field is struggling to identify a second entrant.

Daejon Loves scored 164.72, its second consecutive weekly high, and beat a Manatt team that would have defeated seven of the other eight teams playing this week.

This was not simply a rerun of last week's Jaxon Smith-Njigba explosion. It was a slightly smaller Jaxon Smith-Njigba explosion surrounded by an entire functioning offense.

JSN scored 35.36. Christian McCaffrey added 21.6, James Cook 19.4, Jared Goff 19.36, Deebo Samuel 15.4, Travis Kelce 13.9, Tony Pollard 13.6 and DK Metcalf 12.1.

Every starting offensive player reached double figures.

Dan even left Michael Wilson's 25.9 and Bo Nix's 24.14 on the bench. Apparently the unused draft money also came with a rewards program.

Over the last two weeks, Daejon Loves has scored 333.30 points. Dan is third in the official standings at 2-1, but second in season scoring at 447.94.

The new theory is that he left $37 behind deliberately to give everyone else a chance.

We cannot prove intent. We can confirm that the charitable adjustment has been insufficient.

Jon Manatt, meanwhile, has every right to be annoyed.

The Mixers rebounded from 104.82 to 150.32, the third-highest score of Week 3, and still lost by 14.40.

Garrett Wilson led with 26.7. Jaylen Warren added 20.6, Javonte Williams 18.3, Trey McBride 16.5, Rashee Rice 15.8 and George Pickens 15.2.

That is a good fantasy lineup doing good fantasy lineup things. It simply drew the man currently attempting to disprove the value of money.

Manatt is 1-2, but this was a much better performance than the record will remember.

Final: Daejon Loves 164.72, Manatt Mixers 150.32

Sloan 145.68 · ThunderFunk 133.24

Sloan's three scores: 138.10, 157.68, 145.68.

Three wins. No weekly scoring titles. No particularly convincing evidence of a bad team hiding underneath.

Eric Sloan continues to make being good look suspiciously uneventful.

Brock Purdy scored 31.28, CeeDee Lamb added 20.2, Terry McLaurin contributed 19.7 and the Vikings defense supplied 19. Zay Flowers chipped in 15.4.

Omarion Hampton's 5.6 and Chase Brown's 8.9 kept the running-back questions alive, but Sloan again had enough production elsewhere to cover them.

Puka Nacua remained on the bench with zero. Sloan remained undefeated.

ThunderFunk, unfortunately, is moving in the opposite direction in the standings while improving on the scoreboard.

Tom Ksobiech has gone from 101.46 to 123.66 to 133.24. His record has gone from 0-1 to 0-2 to 0-3.

That is a particularly irritating kind of consistency.

Drake London finally delivered the big receiver week with 28.4. Luther Burden III added 19.8, Kenneth Walker 21.3 and Chuba Hubbard 15.0. But De'Von Achane scored only 1.7.

And then there is the bench.

Brock Bowers scored 27.6 while starting tight end Colston Loveland scored 7.1. That is a 20.5-point difference in a matchup decided by 12.44.

With the benefit of hindsight, that one substitution would have flipped the result. That is arithmetic, not a claim that Tom should have known the future.

It is also the sort of arithmetic that makes opening the app on Tuesday feel like touching a hot stove a second time.

Sloan moves to 3-0. ThunderFunk moves to 0-3 with 427.62 points against, second-most in the league. The schedule has been rough. This week, the winning score was also sitting on the bench.

Final: Sloan 145.68, ThunderFunk 133.24

WE WIN RINGS 116.64 · Sam LaPortaPottys 83.16

Lawrence Moffett has won a game.

The team name remains plural. We are taking this one achievement at a time.

WE WIN RINGS scored 116.64, its best total of the season, and beat Sam LaPortaPottys by 33.48.

Tee Higgins led with 21.0. Lamar Jackson added 20.44, Tyler Warren 14.7 and DJ Moore 12.7. After last week's minus 0.1, Moore has successfully rejoined the positive numbers.

No starter reached 22. Nobody needed to.

Lawrence had faced 177.12 and 157.68 in the first two weeks. This time, the opponent scored 83.16. The schedule finally sent an apology basket.

Josh Ostrem is now 0-3.

The Week 1 bad beat at 141.92 has been followed by 64.12 and 83.16. Those last two weeks combined produced 147.28, barely more than the opener alone.

Kyren Williams tried with 21.8. Tyler Loop scored 12 from the kicker spot, enough to finish as the team's second-highest scorer. Carnell Tate added 11.8.

Drake Maye scored 3.76, Isaiah Likely 3.3, Jalen Coker 3.8 and TreVeyon Henderson 3.9. Saquon Barkley managed 9.0.

Injuries are part of the picture: Nico Collins and Alec Pierce were out on the bench, with A.J. Brown on injured reserve. That helps explain the lineup. It does not make looking at the total any more pleasant.

Sam has scored a league-low 289.20 and allowed a league-high 439.38.

Lowest offense. Most opponent points. A comprehensive refusal by the universe to let Josh enjoy this hobby.

Final: WE WIN RINGS 116.64, Sam LaPortaPottys 83.16

Three Weeks In: What We Actually Know

Estes Park and Sloan are the only teams still unbeaten. Both have earned it.

Dan has turned the league's favorite auction joke into consecutive scoring titles. We have moved from questioning his spending to questioning whether he was deliberately giving everyone a head start.

Cooper is improving. Manatt's latest loss came with a genuinely good score. Hawkeye is still dangerous. Bear has some uncomfortable trends underneath a comfortable-looking record.

Lawrence finally got a manageable opponent and took advantage. Tom keeps scoring more and losing anyway. Josh could use both healthier players and a considerably less hostile schedule.

Three weeks down. Eleven regular-season weeks to go.

There is still plenty of time for the standings to change, the preseason takes to recover, and somebody to beat Dan with a fully funded roster.

Until then, we thank him for his attempted generosity.

The rest of the league respectfully requests that he try harder to make it fair.
