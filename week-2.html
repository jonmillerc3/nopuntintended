REGULAR SEASON · WEEK 2

Turns Out One Week Wasn't Enough Evidence

Matchup breakdowns, awards, receipts, and power rankings.

Two weeks into the season, the league has separated itself into three very distinct groups.

Four teams are undefeated.

Two are 1-1.

Four have begun staring at the standings while telling themselves that 0-2 really isn't that bad.

The Estes Park Rangers followed a 177-point opening week with another 159.68 and remain comfortably atop the league in scoring. Sloan and Hawkeye Inside Her both put up monster weeks to stay perfect. Bear Force One somehow reached 2-0 without looking nearly as dominant as it did a week ago.

And then there is Daejon Loves.

Dan Welter spent the entire preseason getting mocked for leaving $37 of auction money unused, then responded in Week 2 by scoring more points than everybody else.

The money is apparently still sitting there.

So is the 168.58.

This is inconvenient for the editorial staff.

Week 2 Matchups

Estes Park Rangers 159.68 · ThunderFunk 123.66

At some point, this stops being a hot start.

Estes Park followed its league-high 177.12 in Week 1 with 159.68 in Week 2, bringing the defending champion to 336.80 points through two games.

Nobody else is particularly close.

The encouraging part for Jon Miller is that this wasn't simply a replay of Week 1, when Jahmyr Gibbs, Derrick Henry and Ashton Jeanty combined for more than 100 points.

This time the production came from everywhere.

Tyler Shough scored 22.38. Gibbs had 23.3. Chris Olave scored 22.6. Dalton Kincaid added 22.5. Henry contributed 17.7, Parker Washington 16.8 and Christian Watson 14.1.

Seven starters scored at least 14 points.

And just to make the rest of the league more irritated, DeVonta Smith scored 27.7 on the bench.

That's less a lineup problem than a luxury tax issue.

ThunderFunk actually improved substantially from its 101.46-point opener. Dak Prescott scored 29.76, Kenneth Walker added 23.8 and Chase McLaughlin somehow contributed 16 from the kicker spot.

It still wasn't close enough.

The biggest problem continues to be the receiver room. Drake London scored 8.9, Luther Burden III 6.2 and Marvin Harrison Jr. produced zero.

Through two weeks, a team that finished near the top of this league in each of the last two regular seasons is now 0-2.

We have officially moved from “ignore Week 1” to “okay, maybe glance at it.”

Final: Estes Park Rangers 159.68, ThunderFunk 123.66

Hawkeye Inside Her 165.68 · Cooper Dejean Jacket 130.38

Mike Stout scored 92.20 points in Week 1.

So the good news is that Cooper Dejean Jacket improved by almost 40 points.

The bad news is that David Eickholt scored 165.68.

Fantasy football is very supportive like that.

Hawkeye Inside Her got the kind of performance its preseason roster construction was built to produce.

Amon-Ra St. Brown scored 35.2.

Jonathan Taylor scored 29.2.

Ja'Marr Chase rebounded from his 3.2-point opening week with 26.5.

Jake Ferguson contributed another 20.3 from the flex.

That is four players combining for 111.2 points before most of the roster even enters the conversation.

David is now 2-0, and the preseason concern that an expensive top-heavy roster might lack depth suddenly matters a lot less when the expensive guys are doing this.

Mike, meanwhile, deserves some sympathy.

Davante Adams went absolutely nuclear for 39.5 points, Matthew Stafford scored 26.98, and Cooper finished at a perfectly respectable 130.38.

In six of the league's other eight Week 2 team performances, that score would have looked very competitive.

Instead, Mike ran into 165.

Cooper Dejean Jacket is now 0-2, but this was considerably different from Week 1.

Last week looked like a roster problem.

This week looked like a schedule problem.

Final: Hawkeye Inside Her 165.68, Cooper Dejean Jacket 130.38

Bear Force One 122.02 · Manatt Mixers 104.82

There are dominant 2-0 teams.

There are fortunate 2-0 teams.

And then there are teams whose quarterback scores 40.82 points and politely carries everyone else to the finish line.

Welcome, Bear Force One.

Josh Allen accounted for exactly one-third of Chris Shipley's entire score, putting up 40.82 while the rest of the roster mostly behaved like they had dinner reservations.

Bijan Robinson scored 11.1. Jeremiah Love had 7.5. Rhamondre Stevenson 3.6. Kyle Pitts 2.5.

No problem.

Harrison Butker kicked in 16, Tetairoa McMillan added 15.1, and Allen did the heavy lifting.

Bear Force One is 2-0.

Nobody asks how in December.

Jon Manatt's afternoon was considerably less enjoyable.

The Mixers were projected for 142.5.

They scored 104.82.

Caleb Williams produced 7.72. Malik Nabers scored 1.1. The Chiefs defense managed one point.

Trey McBride, Garrett Wilson and Brandon Aubrey tried to keep things respectable, but there simply wasn't enough production elsewhere.

Jalen Hurts scored 16.16 on the bench, which looks annoying until you realize inserting him would still have left Manatt comfortably short.

Sometimes there isn't a lineup decision to blame.

Sometimes your players just don't score.

Very inconsiderate.

Final: Bear Force One 122.02, Manatt Mixers 104.82

Daejon Loves 168.58 · Sam LaPortaPottys 64.12

We need to discuss Dan Welter.

Last week, after Daejon Loves scored 114.64 in a loss, we noted that Dan still had all that unused auction money from the draft.

Very funny.

Everybody laughed.

Dan then scored 168.58, the highest total in the league this week.

Apparently the $37 was earning interest.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba exploded for 42.5 points. Jared Goff scored 29.78. Travis Kelce added 25.1. Christian McCaffrey contributed 22.6 and James Cook another 20.9.

Five players above 20.

That'll do.

Daejon Loves is only 1-1, but here's the more interesting number:

283.22 points scored.

That's fifth in the official standings because of record, but it's actually more total offense than undefeated Bear Force One.

Dan may have been mocked prematurely.

Unfortunately, fantasy journalism provides no mechanism for retracting jokes.

On the other side of the matchup...

Good lord.

Sam LaPortaPottys scored 141.92 points in Week 1 and lost.

Josh Ostrem could reasonably have wondered what he needed to do to catch a break.

The fantasy gods answered:

“Score 64.”

Drake Maye: 8.02.

Saquon Barkley: 3.

Nico Collins: zero.

Alec Pierce: 2.1.

David Montgomery: 4.4.

Chargers defense: 4.

The entire starting lineup combined for 64.12 points.

Daejon Loves had almost matched that by the time JSN, Goff and Kelce were finished.

This wasn't a bad beat.

This was a crime scene with ESPN branding.

Sam LaPortaPottys has gone from the third-highest scorer in Week 1 to the lowest scorer in Week 2.

Fantasy football has range.

Final: Daejon Loves 168.58, Sam LaPortaPottys 64.12

Sloan 157.68 · WE WIN RINGS 111.50

Eric Sloan may be building the quietest problem in the league.

Week 1: 138.10.

Week 2: 157.68.

Record: 2-0.

Total points: 295.78, second only to Estes Park.

CeeDee Lamb erupted for 35.3, Brock Purdy scored 28.48, Rashod Bateman added 21.8, Sam LaPorta had 17.2 and Omarion Hampton 17.5.

Even with Puka Nacua unavailable, Sloan barely noticed.

That is exactly why having an absurdly deep receiver room matters.

Lawrence Moffett, meanwhile, is reaching uncomfortable territory.

WE WIN RINGS entered the season with what looked like one of the league's most balanced rosters.

It is now 0-2.

Jaylen Waddle had a strong 21.8, and Tee Higgins and Tyler Warren both delivered reasonable numbers.

But Lamar Jackson scored only 14.8.

Travis Etienne had 7.1.

And DJ Moore accomplished one of fantasy football's more impressive statistical achievements:

minus 0.1 points.

WE WIN RINGS has now allowed 334.8 points through two weeks, the most in the league.

So Lawrence has both underperformed and been run over by the schedule.

Those things can coexist.

The preseason roster analysis may eventually be vindicated.

At the moment, however, the scoreboard has filed an objection.

Final: Sloan 157.68, WE WIN RINGS 111.50

Two Weeks In: What We Actually Know

We know Estes Park is very good.

We know Sloan's depth is real.

We know Hawkeye Inside Her has enough elite firepower to bury teams when the stars align.

We know Bear Force One can win pretty and can apparently win ugly.

And we now know Daejon Loves probably deserved more respect than it received after the auction.

After that?

We're still guessing.

ThunderFunk has enough recent history that 0-2 shouldn't trigger panic, but another bad week will turn a slow start into an actual hole.

WE WIN RINGS may be considerably better than its record, though eventually the scoreboard requires evidence.

Cooper Dejean Jacket finally looked competitive.

Manatt took a step backward.

And Sam LaPortaPottys managed to score 141.92 one week and 64.12 the next, which is perhaps the clearest possible reminder that nobody actually knows anything.

Two weeks down.

Twelve to go.

And somewhere, Dan Welter still has $37.
