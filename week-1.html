REGULAR SEASON · WEEK 1

The Receipts Have Begun

Five games, five winners, five losers, and exactly one week before the preseason takes started getting dragged into evidence.

One week.

That is all it took for several portions of the preseason analysis to become either remarkably prescient or potentially embarrassing.

The defending champion opened the season by scoring 177.12 points, nearly 23 more than anyone else in the league. Bear Force One looked very much like the contender it was advertised to be. Sam LaPortaPottys scored the third-most points in the league and lost anyway. ThunderFunk, coming off consecutive dominant regular seasons, opened with 101.46. And Cooper Dejean Jacket discovered that having Justin Jefferson score 31 points is considerably less useful when approximately half the rest of the lineup decides not to participate.

Five teams are 1-0. Five teams are 0-1.

Obviously, therefore, the season is basically over and we now know everything.

Week 1 Matchups

The games, the damage, and the first evidence that preseason confidence should always come with an expiration date.

Estes Park Rangers 177.12 · WE WIN RINGS 107.26

The defending champion apparently took the preseason criticism personally.

The preseason concern with Estes Park was pretty straightforward: ridiculous running backs, questionable receiver depth.

The running backs responded by scoring 101.6 points among Jahmyr Gibbs, Derrick Henry and Ashton Jeanty.

Gibbs went for 33.6. Henry scored 35.3. Jeanty added 32.7.

That is obscene.

Then Chris Olave, one of the players supposedly responsible for that questionable receiver room, dropped another 28.2, while $1 keeper Parker Washington contributed 19.3.

Estes scored 177.12 despite getting negative 0.38 points from Kyler Murray.

Read that sentence again.

The quarterback actually subtracted points from the roster, and the Rangers still beat WE WIN RINGS by almost seventy.

For Lawrence Moffett, this was not exactly the debut expected from the team we called one of the most balanced rosters in the league. Breece Hall delivered 19.8 and DJ Moore scored 21, but Jaylen Waddle produced 1.2, Tee Higgins managed 8.9, and Justin Herbert finished at 13.26.

There are losses you analyze. There are losses you learn from. And then there are losses where the other guy scores 177 and you just close the app.

FINAL · ESTES PARK RANGERS 177.12, WE WIN RINGS 107.26

Bear Force One 154.16 · Sam LaPortaPottys 141.92

The best game of Week 1 also produced its cruelest result.

Sam LaPortaPottys scored 141.92 points, the third-highest total in the entire league.

Their reward was an 0-1 record.

Josh Ostrem got 28.9 from David Montgomery, 27.8 from Isaiah Likely, 21.2 from Nico Collins and 15.5 from Kyren Williams. Even kicker Tyler Loop added 14.

Under normal circumstances, that is enough.

Unfortunately, Chris Shipley had Josh Allen.

Allen scored 35.66, Bijan Robinson added 31.3, Bucky Irving put up 20.3 and Mike Evans contributed 16.9 as Bear Force One reached 154.16.

The interesting part is that Bear did this while Kyle Pitts scored zero points.

The preseason writeup said that if Bear Force One's young running backs hit, this might be the best roster in the league. One week is nowhere near enough evidence to make that declaration, but Jeremiah Love produced 13 and Bucky Irving 20.3 behind Bijan's 31.3.

So far, so good.

Meanwhile, Sam LaPortaPottys gets the first official entry into the Fantasy Football Is Stupid file: score 141.92, outscore eight teams in the league, start 0-1.

FINAL · BEAR FORCE ONE 154.16, SAM LAPORTAPOTTYS 141.92

Sloan 138.10 · Daejon Loves 114.64

Eric Sloan spent the offseason building what we called perhaps the deepest receiver room in the league.

Week 1 offered supporting evidence.

Zay Flowers led the way with 26 points, while CeeDee Lamb scored 15.4, Puka Nacua 12.4 and Emeka Egbuka 11.3. None of them individually destroyed the scoreboard, but collectively the depth did exactly what depth is supposed to do.

More importantly, Chase Brown delivered 18.8 and Brock Purdy contributed 21.1, giving Sloan enough production outside the receiver position to cruise to 138.10.

Dan Welter's Daejon Loves had its moments. Jaxon Smith-Njigba scored 26.2, the Steelers defense somehow contributed 19, and Christian McCaffrey gave him 13.8.

But James Cook's 9.9, Tony Pollard's 4.4 and Baker Mayfield's 11.64 left too much ground to make up.

This was not a catastrophic performance from Daejon Loves. At 114.64, Dan would have beaten three other teams this week. He simply ran into a better roster. But hey, he still has all that left over salary cap money from the draft and you don't, so...scoreboard?

FINAL · SLOAN 138.10, DAEJON LOVES 114.64

Manatt Mixers 136.02 · Cooper Dejean Jacket 92.20

The preseason diagnosis for both of these teams was almost comically simple.

Manatt had receivers and questionable running backs.

Cooper Dejean Jacket had receivers and questionable running backs.

Naturally, Manatt won because of a running back.

Javonte Williams scored 24.2 points, joining Jalen Hurts at 24.72 and Trey McBride at 24.5 to give Jon Manatt three players above 24 points.

The rest of the lineup did enough, and the Chiefs defense chipped in 14.

Mike Stout, meanwhile, received exactly what he paid for from the top of his receiver room. Justin Jefferson scored 31.2 and Ladd McConkey added 19.2.

After that, things became unpleasant.

Matthew Stafford: 4.1. George Kittle: 3.2. Davante Adams: 5.6. Courtland Sutton: 3.1. MarShawn Lloyd: 3.7. Texans defense: minus four.

There should probably be a special fantasy rule that if your defense scores negative four points, you are permitted to simply remove them from the lineup and pretend the entire thing never happened.

Cooper Dejean Jacket finished with 92.2, the lowest score in the league.

The preseason concern was whether this roster had enough production outside of its receivers.

Week 1 answer: Not yet.

FINAL · MANATT MIXERS 136.02, COOPER DEJEAN JACKET 92.20

Hawkeye Inside Her 122.26 · ThunderFunk 101.46

This one may have produced the strangest box score of the week.

ThunderFunk got 34.1 points from Kenneth Walker.

And still barely broke 100.

That takes work.

Drake London scored 5.5. Marvin Harrison Jr. scored 4.3. Colston Loveland scored zero. The Rams defense added one. De'Von Achane finished at 10.6.

For a team that has posted the league's best regular-season record in each of the last two seasons, this was not an especially comforting beginning.

David Eickholt didn't exactly receive universal excellence from Hawkeye Inside Her either. Ja'Marr Chase scored just 3.2, Darren Waller had 4.8 and J.K. Dobbins managed 3.6.

But this is why you spend enormous portions of your salary cap on stars.

Amon-Ra St. Brown scored 28.7. Jonathan Taylor scored 25.1. The Seahawks defense added 14.

That was enough.

The preseason criticism of Hawkeye Inside Her was that the top of the roster might be as good as anyone's while the depth behind it could become a problem.

Week 1 was almost a laboratory demonstration of the idea. One superstar disappeared, two others carried the team, and David walked away 1-0.

What We Actually Learned

Probably very little.

It is Week 1.

That's worth remembering before anyone trades half his roster because one player scored four points.

But Week 1 does give us our first pieces of evidence. Estes' absurd running-back investment immediately paid off. Bear Force One looks dangerous. Sloan's receiver depth appears real. Manatt may have more running-back production than expected. Sam LaPortaPottys is considerably better than its 0-1 record suggests.

And at the bottom, ThunderFunk and WE WIN RINGS have enough talent that writing either off after one game would be ridiculous.

Cooper Dejean Jacket?

We'll revisit that one next week.
